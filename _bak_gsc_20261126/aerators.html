Aerators and Air Diffusers

Fine-bubble aeration — the largest energy line in any biological plant.

In short: Fine-bubble aeration, which is the largest energy line in any biological plant. Disc and tubular diffusers are selected against tank depth and grid layout, air supply is matched to the diffuser curve, and lifting arrangements allow service without draining the tank.

Aeration is typically the single biggest electricity consumer in a biological wastewater plant, so the diffuser choice is a running-cost decision, not a capital one. Fine-bubble diffusers transfer oxygen more efficiently than coarse bubbles or surface aerators, but they need clean air and periodic cleaning to stay efficient.

We supply disc and tubular fine-bubble diffusers with the associated pipework and lifting arrangements, and surface aerators where the tank geometry or the duty calls for them.

Common questions

What buyers ask before ordering one.

Why does aeration dominate the power bill?

Because it runs 24 hours and moves the largest mass of air. Fine-bubble diffusers give the highest oxygen transfer efficiency, which is why the diffuser choice decides the blower duty rather than the other way round.

Disc or tubular diffusers?

Selected against tank depth and the aeration grid layout. Shallow tanks and tight grids favour one, deep tanks and long runs favour the other - the selection table gives air flow, resistance loss and service area for each size.

Can diffusers be serviced without emptying the tank?

Yes, with a lifting arrangement - the diffuser is raised to the surface for cleaning while the tank stays full and the plant stays in service.

How is the blower chosen?

From the diffuser curve: resistance loss at the design air flow and water depth sets the blower pressure, so blower selection follows the diffusers, not the other way round.

What decides oxygen transfer?

Water depth, bubble size and service area per diffuser. The table gives oxygen utilisation per size; deeper submersion and finer bubbles raise it.
