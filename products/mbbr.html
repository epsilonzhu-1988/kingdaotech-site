MBBR Media

Moving bed biofilm carriers for capacity upgrades inside existing tanks.

Moving bed biofilm reactor media are small plastic carriers kept in motion by aeration or mixing, each carrying a protected biofilm. Because the biomass grows on carriers rather than as floc, an MBBR can carry far more treatment capacity in the same tank volume than activated sludge — which is why it is the standard answer to a plant that has run out of capacity but not out of space.

The design questions are the fill fraction, the aeration or mixing energy to keep the carriers moving, and the screen that keeps them in the tank.

How MBBR works

Every carrier is a small reactor.

Moving-bed biofilm does not replace the activated sludge — it adds surface for the slow growers that a suspended system washes out.

The mechanism. Carriers are dosed loose into the reactor and kept moving — by aeration in an aerobic tank, by a mixer in an anoxic or anaerobic one. Biofilm grows in the protected internal area of each carrier: anaerobic and facultative organisms inside, aerobic ones on the outer surface. Each piece behaves as a micro-reactor with its own internal gradient.

Why it holds up under load. The biofilm on the carriers adds a large standing biomass on top of the suspended sludge, and it renews continuously as the carriers abrade against each other. The result is a system with more biological mass, faster mass transfer, and far more tolerance of a shock load than a suspended-growth tank of the same volume.

What it is good for. Upgrading an existing tank without adding volume, nitrogen removal where the sludge age must be long, and industrial effluent with a load that swings.
