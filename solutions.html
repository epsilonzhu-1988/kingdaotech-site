Solutions by Wastewater Type

The wastewater decides the process — not the machine we want to sell.

Twelve wastewater types we have designed plants for. Each one has a route behind it, and a reference you can check.

In short: Twelve wastewater types, each with a process route behind it and a reference you can check. The route is chosen from the effluent data first - flow, organic load, salinity, oil and the discharge standard - and the equipment follows from the route. If a conventional process meets your standard at lower cost, we say so instead of selling a membrane.

01Municipal wastewater Challenge: Nitrogen and phosphorus limits, seasonal load variation, and a discharge permit that does not move. Typical route: Inverted AAO or modified Bardenpho → high-density clarifier → filtration → disinfection. 50,000 m³/d Xingyang · 15,000 m³/d Sihanoukville SEZ · 900 m³/d Cifeng (Pengzhou) · 100,000 m³/d Xinzheng Longhu (staged)
02Tannery and leather Challenge: Chromium, sulphide, high salinity and high ammonia — the four that break a conventional plant. Typical route: Chrome and sulphide separated at source → equalisation → coagulation-sedimentation → A+B oxidation ditch. 20,000 m³/d Dhaka Tannery Estate, Bangladesh
03Textile, dyeing and printing Challenge: High colour, high salinity, PVA desizing water and poor biodegradability. Typical route: Segregated desizing and dyeing streams → coagulation/DAF → hydrolysis-acidification → AAO → reuse at 50%. 35,000 m³/d Shanquan (50% reuse) · Vietnam industrial park
04PCB and electronics Challenge: Copper, nickel, cyanide and chelated metals across several process lines. Typical route: Stream segregation → chemical reduction and precipitation per stream → DAF + Fenton → aerobic biological polishing. 20,000 m³/d Suichuan Phase I · 30,000 m³/d Phase II
05Electroplating Challenge: Chelated metals that will not precipitate with simple lime dosing. Typical route: Batch segregation by metal → reduction/precipitation → ion exchange or membrane concentration where recovery is required. Vietnam industrial park · 400 m³/d mixed plating stream
06Slaughterhouse Challenge: High oil, high blood content, high COD and a B/C ratio that fluctuates with the kill schedule. Typical route: Grease separation → equalisation → anoxic-aerobic → dewatering. 1,200 t/d Changshu · 700 t/d Zhangjiagang
07Livestock farming Challenge: High ammonia, high suspended solids, strong odour, and a site that is often far from any sewer. Typical route: Solid-liquid separation → anaerobic digestion (optional) → A/O → polishing. 280 m³/d Sansheng · 250 m³/d Xinpu
08Down and feather processing Challenge: Feather, fine solids and surfactant load from washing lines, mixed with park domestic sewage. Typical route: Screening and grit removal → equalisation → coagulation → biological treatment → dewatering. 20,000 m³/d Lu'an Guzhen (20+ factories in one park)
09Water reuse Challenge: A reuse target that has to be met every day, not on average. Typical route: Biological treatment → membrane or filtration → disinfection; staged by quality tier so industrial and landscape demand are served separately. 30,000 m³/d Karamay Baijiantan · 50% reuse at Shanquan
10Water supply treatment Challenge: Seasonal turbidity spikes and a turbidity limit below what conventional filtration reliably holds. Typical route: Inclined plate settling → V-filter → immersed ultrafiltration. 50,000 m³/d Nanzhao No.2 waterworks; turbidity <0.1 NTU
11River and waterbody rehabilitation Challenge: Diffuse pollution, low dissolved oxygen and no space for a conventional plant. Typical route: Packaged AO fixed-bed units with grit separation, phosphorus removal and enhanced oxidation. 300 m³/d Puyuan Road river restoration
12Industrial park and SEZ Challenge: Mixed tenants, mixed streams, one discharge permit — and a drainage tariff that has to recover the investment. Typical route: Stream segregation by tenant type → shared biological train → reuse where justified; plant sized against the tariff model. Sihanoukville SEZ (Cambodia) · Suichuan Industrial Park · Vietnam industrial park

Common questions

What engineers ask before sending us data.

How do you choose the process route?

From the effluent, not from the equipment. Four steps: read the wastewater type, set the route that has already worked for that type, size it against your flow and load, then check it against your discharge standard and reuse target.

What information do you need to propose a route?

Flow (average and peak), COD, BOD, suspended solids, oil and grease, salinity or conductivity where relevant, pH, temperature, and the discharge or reuse standard you have to meet. Any existing tank volume matters too if it is a retrofit.

Which wastewater types have references behind them?

Twelve - municipal, tannery and leather, textile dyeing and printing, PCB and electronics, electroplating, chemical and pharmaceutical, food and slaughterhouse, livestock farming, down and feather, landfill leachate, reuse, and packaged small plants. Each has at least one delivered reference listed on the projects page.

Will you tell me if a membrane is the wrong choice?

Yes. If a conventional process meets your standard at lower cost, we will say so - the process is chosen from the effluent, not from the machine we would prefer to sell.

Can the same route serve a retrofit and a new build?

Usually yes, with different civil works. A retrofit is constrained by the tanks that already exist, so the route is fitted to the volume available rather than to the ideal footprint.
